## The short answer

**Target since v1.1: three months per charge.** ECO mode reaches **3.0
months** on a 4000 mAh cell with a 25 % engineering margin, and 3.8 months
without the margin. That includes a dashboard reading the sensor every 15
minutes over Matter.

It gets there with particulate matter every **hour** and VOC every 10 s as a
tripwire: a rising VOC index switches the device into ACTIVE (particles every
2 min) within about two minutes.

NORMAL mode (particles every 15 min) lasts about three weeks. ACTIVE would
empty the battery in three days, which is why it is entered automatically, is
time-limited and falls back on its own.

The v1.0 target was six months with particles every four hours. That setting
still exists as `ECO_LONG` (6.0 months with margin) and is one configuration
value away. See *What would change these numbers* below.

## Why the particle sensor sets the pace

One 40 s SPS30 window costs about 0.9 mAh. At one window an hour that is
**21 mAh a day, two thirds of the whole budget**. Everything else combined
(radio, VOC, CO2, fuel gauge, LED, self-discharge) is about 10.5 mAh.

That is why no amount of radio tuning changes the headline much, and why the
design watches **VOC** continuously instead: at 1.5 mAh/day it is the
tripwire, and the expensive particle channel only speeds up when the tripwire
fires. Printing PLA, PETG, ABS or ASA raises the VOC index well before an
hourly particle sample would notice anything.

The honest limitation: a short particle event that produces no VOC (sweeping,
a dusty draught) can fall between two hourly samples.

| particles every 2 h | about 4.5 months |
| 5000 mAh cell instead of 4000 | +25 %, but the case gets 12 mm taller |
| PM window 30 s instead of 40 s | about 3.6 months; shorter than Sensirion's recommended 30 s settle plus averaging |
| Long Idle Time ICD (experimental) | radio from 2.9 to under 1 mAh/day: about +6 % |
| dashboard reads every 5 min instead of 15 | about −3 % |
| Thread TX power 14 dBm instead of 20 | about +2 % |

Nothing here has been measured on an assembled AIR CHECK, because none has
been built. Two things to measure first with a PPK2:

1. **Idle floor.** The model says about 100 µA (MCU floor + carrier). If it
   reads 1 mA or more, the Feather's WS2812B is powered. That means GPIO20 is
   being held high, which EDR-11 is about.
2. **One full ECO hour**, particle window included. The model says 31.5 mAh/day,
   i.e. an average of about 1.3 mA.

ECO sits almost exactly on the three-month line with margin. A carrier that
comes in 50 % worse than estimated still leaves 2.9 months; at 150 µA it is
2.8. If the real
figures come in worse than that, `ECO_LONG` or a 2 h particle interval
recovers it without any hardware change.
