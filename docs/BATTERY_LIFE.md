## The short answer

**Since v1.4 the device runs from USB-C with a LiFePO4 backup** (EDR-21):
four AER18650m2A2 cells in parallel (1S4P), charged in the device by the
Power-Standard's module C. On a USB-C charger it runs indefinitely; after a
full charge the firmware pauses charging and tops the cells up about once a
month.

On the cells alone, ECO mode - particles every hour, CO2 every 5 min,
temperature, humidity and VOC every 10 s - with a 25 % engineering margin:

| pack | runtime |
|---|---|
| **1S4P LiFePO4 (fitted)** | **2.8 months** (3.5 nominal, 2.4 in the worst case) |
| 1S2P, for comparison | 1.5 months |
| 1S1P, for comparison | 0.8 months |

The battery session's rougher estimate for general use is about 67 days.
The least certain input is the cells' self-discharge (3 %/month assumed, no
manufacturer figure): at 1.5 %/month ECO reaches 3.0 months, at 5 % 2.7.

Charging from flat takes 8-9 h (the device's own draw shares the 1.1 A input
limit; one safety-timer restart is allowed, EDR-21). While charging,
temperature and humidity can read a little high; Matter's `BatChargeState`
marks that window.

3.3 V loads are divided by both efficiencies (0.85 x 0.90 = 0.765); the Sunrise and the LED only by the regulator's. The regulator's quiescent current is charged at the cell voltage on top of that, which double-counts some of its light-load loss - the conservative direction. The regulator boosts the 3.0-3.65 V of the cells to 3.90 V; its 85 % is the bottom of Pololu's band and has not been measured at this operating point.

## On USB-C

On USB-C the module feeds the device from the charger (power path); the cells lose only the charger's and the BMS's quiescent current, the cell voltage divider and their own self-discharge. After a full charge the firmware pauses charging (CE) until the cells drop below 3.30 V, were used, or 30 days have passed - so on permanent USB-C the cells are topped up about once a month and are always close to full when the power goes.
