## The short answer

**Since v1.3 the device runs from 6 × AA.** Nothing is charged inside the
device (EDR-18); the cells are swapped, or recharged in an ordinary charger.

In ECO mode - particles every hour, CO2 every 5 min, temperature, humidity
and VOC every 10 s - with a 25 % engineering margin:

| cells | runtime |
|---|---|
| **Energizer Ultimate Lithium L91** (recommended) | **3.7 months** (3.1 in the worst case) |
| eneloop pro (NiMH, rechargeable) | 2.2 months |
| alkaline | 2.1 months |
| 1.5 V Li-ion with USB-C | 1.8 months - not recommended |

The headline target (three months) is met with L91 cells, also in the worst
case. With NiMH or alkaline cells, a particle window every 2 h gives 3.3 and
3.1 months (`config set` - see CONFIGURATION.md).

**A correction to the v1.3 proposal.** Before the model was rewritten the
proposal quoted 4.3 / 2.5 / 2.6 months. The model now charges the Pololu
regulator's quiescent current (< 0.2 mA, Pololu's only figure) at the full
pack voltage for 24 hours a day - about a fifth of the ECO budget - and the
numbers above are the result.

## Why the SEN62 sets the pace

One 60 s SEN62 window costs about 5.5 mWh at the cells. At one window an
hour that is **133 mWh a day, two thirds of the ECO budget**. The CO2 sensor,
in contrast, costs well under 1 mWh a day even every 5 minutes - the Sunrise
takes 1.6 mC per measurement - so CO2 no longer has to share the particle
clock (it did in v1.2).

The honest limitation: a short particle event that produces no VOC
(sweeping, a dusty draught) can fall between two hourly samples. Printing
PLA, PETG, ABS or ASA raises the VOC index first, and that switches the
device to ACTIVE.

## What would change these numbers

ECO on L91 cells, with margin (baseline 3.7 months):

| change | effect |
|---|---|
| particle window every 2 h | **5.6 months** (NiMH 3.3, alkaline 3.1) |
| particle window every 30 min | 2.3 months |
| CO2 every 10 min instead of 5 | no visible change |
| regulator quiescent 0.4 mA instead of 0.2 | 3.1 months |
| a regulator with ~11 µA quiescent (TPS63802 class) | 4.6 months |
| FireBeetle board quiescent 150 µA instead of 29 | 3.5 months |
| board quiescent 300 µA | 3.3 months |
| dashboard reads every 5 min instead of 15 | 3.7 months (−2 %) |

| line | status |

| SEN62 currents | **datasheet**: SEN6x v0.92, Table 11, SEN62 column (75 mA typ, 90 mA max after the first 60 s) |
| Sunrise | **datasheet**: Senseair TDE7318, 1.60 mC per 32-sample measurement |
| SGP40, SHT40 | **vendor published**; both breakouts have no regulator, the SparkFun LED jumper is cut |
| ESP32-C6 ICD at a 15 s poll | **independently measured** (PPK2, one hour, Microamp Home) |
| FireBeetle quiescent 29 µA | **vendor figure** minus the chip - the least certain line |
| Pololu S9V11E2A | **vendor figure** "< 0.2 mA for most combinations"; no light-load curve published |
| cell energies | **datasheets**; the 1.5 V Li-ion line is an assumption from independent tests |
| dashboard reads | **assumption**: 96 a day at 10 poll-equivalents each |

Nothing here has been measured on an assembled AIR CHECK. Measure first
(TESTING.md, T-E0..T-E3) - the regulator's quiescent current with a µA meter
in the pack lead, everything else with a PPK2 in the 4.0 V line (its
ammeter mode stops at 5 V, below the pack voltage):

0. **Regulator quiescent**, output unloaded: < 0.2 mA per Pololu.
1. **Idle floor**, SEN62 off, Thread attached. The model says about
   2.8 mW at the cells (≈ 0.33 mA at 8.7 V), of which the regulator's
   quiescent current is the biggest part.
2. **One full ECO hour**, SEN62 window included: model 8.4 mWh.
3. **One SEN62 window** on its own: model 5.5 mWh at the cells.

If the regulator's quiescent current comes in worse, a 2 h particle window
recovers it without any hardware change.
