## The short answer

**ECO mode reaches 6.4 months on a 4000 mAh cell with a 25 % engineering
margin applied.** It gets there by sampling particulate matter every four
hours, which is a real compromise and is explained under *Why ECO samples PM
so rarely* below.

NORMAL mode - the one to use on a device standing next to a running printer -
lasts about three weeks. ACTIVE mode, which the device enters by itself when
it detects an emission event, would flatten the battery in three days, which
is exactly why it is time limited and falls back on its own.

## Why ECO samples PM so rarely

The SPS30 is not a little bit more expensive than the other sensors, it is
*two orders of magnitude* more expensive. One 40 s measurement window costs
about 0.9 mAh. Everything else the device does in a whole day - the radio, the
VOC channel, the CO2 channel, the display, the quiescent current of the board -
adds up to roughly 9 mAh. Four PM measurements a day and the SPS30 is already
the largest single line in the budget.

So the design does not try to watch particles continuously. It watches **VOC**
continuously, every 10 seconds, for 1.5 mAh a day, and uses that as the
tripwire:

```
ECO: VOC every 10 s  ->  VOC rises above baseline  ->  ACTIVE within ~2 min
                                                        PM every 2 min
```

Printing PLA, PETG, ABS or ASA raises the VOC index long before a four-hourly
PM sample would have noticed anything, so in practice the device does not miss
print events - it misses *short particle events that produce no VOC*, such as
someone shaking out a dust cloth in the next room. That is the honest
limitation, and it is the price of the six month figure.

If you would rather have the particle channel watching continuously, set the
mode to NORMAL and expect to charge the device every three weeks. Both are one
setting, and neither requires different hardware.

## What would change these numbers

| change | effect |
|---|---|
| 5000 mAh cell instead of 4000 | +25 % everywhere; needs a 12 mm deeper case |
| PM window 60 s instead of 40 s | ECO drops to about 5.6 months |
| PM every 2 h instead of 4 h | ECO drops to about 4.8 months |
| VOC every 1 s instead of 10 s | costs about 14 mAh/day, ECO drops to under 2 months |
| LIT ICD instead of SIT (experimental) | saves roughly 1.5 mAh/day, about +10 % |
| Thread TX power 14 dBm instead of 20 | saves roughly 0.6 mAh/day |
| an ESP32-H2 instead of the C6 | Espressif measure 104 uA against 174 uA at the same poll rate: about +7 % |

Nothing in this table has been measured on an assembled AIR CHECK, because no
AIR CHECK has been assembled. The number to distrust first is the carrier
quiescent current: at 62 uA it is 1.5 mAh/day, and if the real board lands at
150 uA instead, ECO falls from 6.4 months to about 5.3. `docs/TESTING.md`
describes how to measure it with a PPK II before you trust the headline.
