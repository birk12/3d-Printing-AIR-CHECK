## The short answer

**Target: three months per charge.** Since v1.2 ECO mode reaches **3.2
months** on a 4000 mAh cell with a 25 % engineering margin, and 4.0 months
without the margin. That includes a dashboard reading the sensor every 15
minutes over Matter. If every SEN63C window drew the datasheet *maximum*
current instead of the typical one, it would be 2.7 months with margin.

It gets there with particles, CO2, temperature and humidity every **hour**
(one 40 s SEN63C window) and VOC every 10 s as a tripwire: a rising VOC index
switches the device into ACTIVE (a window every 2 min) within about two
minutes.

NORMAL mode (a window every 15 min) lasts about three weeks. ACTIVE would
empty the battery in three days, which is why it is entered automatically, is
time-limited and falls back on its own.

**A correction to v1.1.** The v1.1 figure of 3.0 months was too high. Its
model missed the LDO and power LED on both Adafruit sensor breakouts (about
185 µA each, permanently powered) and under-counted the load switch; the real
figure would have been about 2.3 months. EDR-14 has the details, and v1.2
switches the remaining breakout off between samples.

## Why the SEN63C sets the pace

One 40 s SEN63C window costs about 0.9 mAh. At one window an hour that is
**21 mAh a day, over two thirds of the whole budget**. Everything else combined
(radio, VOC, board, LED, self-discharge) is about 8 mAh.

That is why no amount of radio tuning changes the headline much, and why the
design watches **VOC** continuously instead: at under 1 mAh/day it is the
tripwire, and the expensive channel only speeds up when the tripwire fires.
Printing PLA, PETG, ABS or ASA raises the VOC index well before an hourly
particle sample would notice anything.

The honest limitations: a short particle event that produces no VOC
(sweeping, a dusty draught) can fall between two hourly samples, and since
v1.2 CO2 is measured on the same hourly clock as the particles.

Nothing here has been measured on an assembled AIR CHECK, because none has
been built. Three things to measure first with a PPK2:

1. **Idle floor**, both sensor rails off, Thread attached. The model says about
   70 µA (39 µA chip floor in ICD sleep plus 29 µA board plus the switches).
   C6 boards have measured far worse than their datasheets before
   (EDR-13: 226-549 µA on other boards), and the table above shows what
   150 or 300 µA would cost.
2. **One full ECO hour**, SEN63C window included. The model says 29.6 mAh/day,
   an average of about 1.23 mA.
3. **One SEN63C window on its own**, to replace the datasheet's
   "after 60 s" current with what a 40 s window really draws.

If the real figures come in worse, a 2 h window or `ECO_LONG` recovers it
without any hardware change.
